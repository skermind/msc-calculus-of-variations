
### Theorem 3.3
Let $\Omega$ be a bounded open set with Lipschitz boundary. Let $f \in C^0(\overline{\Omega} \times \mathbb{R} \times \mathbb{R}^n)$, $f = f(x,u,\xi)$ satisfy,
$$
\begin{aligned}
(H1)\quad &\xi \mapsto f(x,u,\xi)
\text{ is convex for every }
(x,u)\in\overline{\Omega}\times\mathbb{R},\\[0.5em]
(H2)\quad &\text{There exist }
p>q\ge1,\;
\alpha_1>0,\;
\alpha_2,\alpha_3\in\mathbb{R}
\text{ such that}\\
&f(x,u,\xi)
\ge
\alpha_1|\xi|^p
+\alpha_2|u|^q
+\alpha_3,
\qquad
\forall (x,u,\xi)\in
\overline{\Omega}\times\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^n.
\end{aligned}
$$
Let
$$
(P) \quad \inf \left\{ I(u) = \int_\Omega f(x,u(x), \nabla u(x)) \, dx : u \in u_0 + W_0^{1,p} \right\} = m
$$
where $u_0\in W^{1,p}(\Omega)$ with $I(u_0) < \infty$. Then there exists $\overline{u} \in u_0 + W^{1,p}_0(\Omega)$ a minimiser of $(P)$.
Furthermore if $(u,\xi) \rightarrow f(x,u,\xi)$ is strictly convex for every $x \in \overline{\Omega}$, then the minimiser is unique.

> [!summary] The two key hypotheses of the Direct Method
>
> **(H1) Convexity**
>
> $$
> \xi \mapsto f(x,u,\xi)\text{ is convex.}
> $$
>
> - Also called **convexity in the gradient variable**.
> - Ensures the functional is **weakly lower semicontinuous**.
> - This allows the weak limit of a minimising sequence to remain a minimiser.
>
> ---
>
> **(H2) Coercivity**
>
> $$
> f(x,u,\xi)
> \ge
> \alpha_1|\xi|^p+\alpha_2|u|^q+\alpha_3.
> $$
>
> - Also called the **coercive growth condition**.
> - Ensures the energy becomes large when \(u\) or \(\nabla u\) become large.
> - Prevents minimising sequences from "escaping to infinity".
> - Gives boundedness in the Sobolev norm, allowing compactness to be applied.
>
> **Memory aid:**
> - **H1 keeps the minimum when passing to the limit.**
> - **H2 keeps the sequence under control.**

> [!tip] Why is the same \(p\) used in coercivity and Sobolev spaces?
>
> The exponent \(p\) in the coercivity assumption
>
> $$
> f(x,u,\xi)
> \ge
> \alpha_1|\xi|^p+\cdots
> $$
>
> determines how strongly the energy controls the gradient.
>
> Since
>
> $$
> I(u)=\int_\Omega f(x,u,\nabla u)\,dx,
> $$
>
> bounded energy implies
>
> $$
> \int_\Omega |\nabla u|^p\,dx<\infty,
> $$
>
> which is exactly the condition
>
> $$
> \nabla u\in L^p(\Omega).
> $$
>
> Therefore the natural space for the minimisation problem is
>
> $$
> W^{1,p}(\Omega).
> $$
>
> **Examples**
> - $\(f(\xi)=\tfrac12|\xi|^2\) ⟶ \(p=2\) ⟶ work in \(W^{1,2}(\Omega)\)$.
> -$\(f(\xi)=\sqrt{1+|\xi|^2}\) ⟶ grows like \(|\xi|\) ⟶ \(p=1\) ⟶ work in \(W^{1,1}(\Omega)\).$

> [!tip] How are the constants \(\alpha_1,\alpha_2,\alpha_3\) chosen?
>
> The constants are **not unique**. They are simply values that make the coercivity inequality
>
> $$
> f(x,u,\xi)
> \ge
> \alpha_1|\xi|^p
> +
> \alpha_2|u|^q
> +
> \alpha_3
> $$
>
> true for all \((x,u,\xi)\).
>
> ### Strategy
> 1. Identify how \(f\) grows as \(|\xi|\to\infty\).
> 2. Choose \(p\) to match this growth.
> 3. Pick any constants \(\alpha_1,\alpha_2,\alpha_3\) that provide a valid lower bound.
>
> The goal is **not** to find the best constants, only to prove that such constants exist.
>
> **Examples**
> - \(\tfrac12|\xi|^2\): choose \(\alpha_1=\tfrac12,\ \alpha_2=0,\ \alpha_3=0\).
> - \(\sqrt{1+|\xi|^2}\): use \(\sqrt{1+t^2}\ge t\), so choose \(\alpha_1=1,\ p=1,\ \alpha_2=0,\ \alpha_3=0\).
>

### Example 3.5
The Dirichlet integral satisfies all the hypotheses with $p =2$. The generalisation of the Dirichlet integral is, where $g$ is continuous and non negative and $p>1$
$$
f(x,u,\xi) = \frac{1}{p}|\xi|^p +g(x,u)
$$

### Example 3.6
The minimal surface problem has an integrand given by
$$
f(x,u,\xi) = f(\xi) = \sqrt{1+|\xi|^2}
$$
satisfies all but $(H2)$, this is only verified at $p=1$. But this creates the Sobolev space $W^{1,1}$ which is not reflexive.

> [!tip] Why is reflexivity important?
>
> The Direct Method needs the following implication:
>
> $$
> \text{bounded sequence}
> \Longrightarrow
> \text{weakly convergent subsequence}.
> $$
>
> This is guaranteed when the Sobolev space is **reflexive**.
>
> ---
>
> ### Key fact
>
> $$
> W^{1,p}(\Omega)
> \text{ is reflexive whenever }
> 1<p<\infty.
> $$
>
> However,
>
> $$
> W^{1,1}(\Omega)
> \text{ is not reflexive.}
> $$
>
> ---
>
> ### Why is this a problem?
>
> For the minimal surface functional,
>
> $$
> f(\xi)=\sqrt{1+|\xi|^2},
> $$
>
> the energy only grows like
>
> $$
> |\xi|,
> $$
>
> so coercivity holds only with \(p=1\).
>
> The natural space becomes \(W^{1,1}(\Omega)\), which is not reflexive. Therefore, a bounded minimising sequence may **not** have a weakly convergent subsequence, so the compactness step of the Direct Method breaks down.
>
> **Memory aid:**  
> Reflexivity is what turns **boundedness** into **weak convergence**.

### Example 3.7
The weakening of $(H2)$ leads to the following counterexample

Let $n=1$, $f(x,u,\xi) =f(y,\xi) =\sqrt{u^2+\xi^2}$ over $(0,1)$ with boundarys $u(0) =0, u(1) =1$. with $u\in W^{1,1}(0,1)$

Fist, show that $m=1$ we start by observing that $m\ge 1$. 
$$
I(u) \ge \int^1_0 |u'(x)|\, dx \ge \int^1_0 u'(x)\, dx = u(1) -u(0) =1
$$

To establish that $m=1$ we construct a minimising sequence $u_\nu$. 

$$
u_\nu(x)=
\begin{cases}
0, & \text{if } x \in \left[0,1-\frac{1}{\nu}\right],\\[0.5em]
1+\nu(x-1), & \text{if } x \in \left[1-\frac{1}{\nu},1\right].
\end{cases}
$$

Therefore $m =1$ since
$$
1 \le I(u_\nu) = \int^1_{1-\frac{1}{\nu}} \sqrt{(1+\nu(x-1))^2 +\nu^2} \, dx \le \frac{1}{\nu}\sqrt{1+\nu^2} \rightarrow 1, \text{ as }\nu \rightarrow \infty
$$

Assume for the sake of contradiction there exists $\overline{u}$ a minimiser of $(P)$. We have
$$
1 = I(\overline{u}) = \int^1_0 \sqrt{\overline{u}^2 + \overline{u}'^2} \, dx \ge \int^1_0 \overline{u}\, dx = \overline{u}(1) - \overline{u}(0) =1 
$$
this implies that $\overline{u} =0$ in $(0,1)$. $\overline{u} \equiv 0$ is incompatible with boundary data. Thus $(P)$ has no solution.
> [!warning] Example 3.7 — failure of weakened coercivity
>
> The integrand
>
> $$
> f(u,\xi)=\sqrt{u^2+\xi^2}
> $$
>
> has only **linear growth**, so coercivity holds only at the borderline case \(p=1\).
>
> However, unlike standard (H2), it does not separately control \(u\) and \(\nabla u\).
>
> ---
>
> ### Key consequence
> Minimising sequences can concentrate (form boundary layers), so compactness fails.
>
> ---
>
> ### Why no minimiser exists
>
> Assuming a minimiser \(\overline{u}\),
>
> $$
> I(\overline{u}) \ge \int_0^1 |\overline{u}'|dx \ge 1.
> $$
>
> Equality forces \(\overline{u}'=0\) a.e., hence \(\overline{u}\equiv 0\),
> which contradicts the boundary condition.
>
> ---
>
> ### Main lesson
> Weak coercivity (linear growth) destroys compactness → the Direct Method breaks down.
